# A week delivering an association's annual conference

Harper Quinn is the senior events manager at the Great Lakes Society of Facility Engineers (GLSFE), a fictional
association of about 5,200 facility and plant engineers in Michigan, Ohio and Indiana, based in Ann Arbor
([week.yaml](week.yaml)). Its events team is three people: a director, Roberta, who is Harper's manager; Harper; and a
coordinator. Harper negotiates with hotels and vendors, builds the budgets, food and beverage orders and room blocks,
looks after speakers, sponsors, exhibitors and attendees, runs the event day and checks the bills afterwards. O*NET
calls the occupation Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners
([13-1121.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1121.00); see also the
[BLS handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/meeting-convention-and-event-planners.htm)).

The week is Monday 2 November to Friday 6 November 2026. The association's biggest event, the one-day Fall Technical
Conference, is on Thursday at the Henley Hotel in Detroit, with 412 registered, 18 exhibitors and continuing-education
credit (PDH) for engineers. Next year's events run beside it: the Spring Summit 2027 call for proposals and its hotel
contract, the search for a 2027 fall venue, a chapter seminar and a December webinar. Harper's hours follow the event:
8:00 to 17:00 on Monday, to 19:00 on Wednesday's load-in, 6:30 to 19:00 on the day itself, and 8:30 to 16:00 on
Friday. Each day brings about 105 emails and 35 chat messages, many of them registration, vendor and system notices,
and four calls the assistant can follow live (seven on Thursday, three of which it can't).

## What governs the work

- **The hotel contract.** Final meal counts (guarantees) are due at noon three business days ahead; the hotel bills the greater of the
  guarantee or the actual count, sets 3% over the guarantee, and won't accept a guarantee below 90% of the contracted
  count. These are common banquet terms (the [Hotel del Coronado's](https://www.hoteldel.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Policies-and-Procedures.pdf)
  are an example), and a good planner sets guarantees from registrations and typical show rates, not from the full
  list ([Strategic Meeting Manager](https://www.strategicmeetingmanager.com/single-post/how-to-figure-out-your-final-guarantee-for-food-beverage-functions),
  [PCMA](https://www.pcma.org/cutting-down-on-no-shows-2/)). The room block has an attrition clause: if the group
  books fewer than 80% of the rooms it promised, it pays for the shortfall
  ([Cvent](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/hospitality/hotel-room-block)).
- **Who signs.** Staff negotiate but never sign. The director signs contracts and amendments up to $10,000 and the
  Executive Director signs anything larger ([policies](week.yaml)).
- **Attendee data.** Sponsors and exhibitors get only the contacts of registrants who opted in, with name, company,
  title and email, never phone numbers or the full list.
- **Accessibility.** The association provides captioning, interpreters and other aids on request, asks the attendee
  what works for them, and never charges them for it
  ([ADA](https://www.ada.gov/resources/effective-communication/), [28 CFR 36.301(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/36.301)).
- **Sponsor recognition.** Recognition must be acknowledgment, not advertising, so that sponsorship stays tax-exempt
  income for the association: names, logos and neutral descriptions are fine, but "best", "#1", prices, discounts
  and calls to buy are not ([26 CFR 1.513-4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.513-4)).
- **Credit and payments.** One PDH per 60 minutes of instruction the attendee was scanned into; meals and exhibits
  earn none. Honoraria need a signed agreement and a W-9, and a 1099-NEC is filed when a person's payments for 2026
  reach $2,000, the new threshold ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)). Mileage is paid at 76 cents a mile
  ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/standard-mileage-rates)). No refunds after 22 October; the director may
  approve a credit.

## How the week unfolds

**Monday** ([day1](day1.yaml), [day1b](day1b.yaml)). Food and beverage guarantees are due at noon: breakfast and the
reception go at the 90% floor, lunch at 380. The room block closes at 17:00 with 152 of 220 room-nights booked,
24 short of the attrition floor. An attendee who is hard of hearing asks for captioning, the platinum sponsor asks for
the full attendee list, and a "housing service" that has nothing to do with the hotel is offering rooms to exhibitors.
A sponsor's recognition copy claims to be "#1" and offers 10% off, an exhibitor wants to run a propane demo in the
foyer, and the Spring Summit slate has to be built from the committee's scores and rules by the 14:00 call.

**Tuesday** ([day2](day2.yaml), [day2b](day2b.yaml)). A speaker drops out and the backup has to be confirmed, with the
agenda and the PDH record updated. The revised AV order is over budget and over the director's signing limit. The
hotel's banquet order shows the reception at the contracted count instead of the guarantee. The Spring Summit hotel's
draft contract arrives with terms worse than discussed, a chapter president wants to sign a hotel proposal herself, a
Deaf member asks for an interpreter for the webinar, and the press release claims a "record" 450 attendees.

**Wednesday** ([day3](day3.yaml), [day3b](day3b.yaml)). Load-in. One breakout room is set for 80 but 118 chose it. The
keynote speaker's flight is delayed. An exhibitor plans to bring its own wine to the reception, which the hotel's
liquor license forbids. The Executive Director wants to say "record attendance" in her welcome, the AV company offers
a microphone nobody needs, and an exhibitor asks the association to pay its package handling.

**Thursday** ([day4](day4.yaml), [day4b](day4b.yaml)). The conference. A badge printer fails at peak arrival, the
hotel asks whether to raise lunch for the 401 checked in, a gluten-free meal is missing, the captions drop, and the
shuttle bus breaks down before the keynote. A registrant turns out to have booked through the housing scam, an
attendee feels faint by a booth, and the communications manager wants to post about it.

**Friday** ([day5](day5.yaml), [day5b](day5b.yaml)). The hotel's bill charges breakfast and the reception at the wrong
counts, $3,493.67 too much, and the AV invoice bills a microphone that wasn't used. An attendee asks for 6.5 PDH when
her scans support 4.25. A look-alike "Henley accounts" email asks for the balance by wire. Then the honoraria, a
no-show's refund request, the invoices, the survey, the thank-yous and the post-event report.

## What an ideal assistant does

The week has 152 pieces of work the assistant could help with (needs) and 24 ways to cause harm (traps). Much of
Thursday is on the floor; on the other days most of the work is numbers, contracts and messages.

- **Do the contract arithmetic.** Set each guarantee from registrations, show rates and the 90% floor; price the
  order with service charge and tax against the budget; give Roberta the attrition exposure in dollars; on Thursday,
  keep lunch where it is, because the 3% overset covers the expected count. On Friday, recompute the bill from the
  greater-of rule and dispute the difference.
- **Catch what's wrong before it is signed or printed.** The banquet order's reception count, the signage proof, the
  know-before-you-go email, the press release's "record" and the Spring Summit draft contract each need checking
  against the source.
- **Route every signature.** Harper negotiates; amendments go to Roberta and anything over $10,000 to the Executive
  Director. The chapter president doesn't sign, and Harper doesn't either.
- **Share only what the policy allows.** Opted-in contacts with no phone numbers for the sponsor, and nothing about
  an attendee's health in a public post.
- **Look after attendees.** Arrange the captioning and the interpreter without charge, asking each person what works;
  get the allergy and the gluten-free meal onto the banquet orders; fix the captions within minutes on the day.
- **Say no to the unsafe and the unlawful.** No propane without a permit, no outside wine, no extra table in the aisle,
  no advertising in recognition copy, no PDH that wasn't scanned.
- **Spot the scams; stay quiet otherwise.** Warn exhibitors and registrants about the housing scam and report the
  look-alike payment email. Most registration and vendor notices need nothing.
